The finding · a target shortlist

Five candidate brakes, ranked by convergent evidence.

A T-cell "brake" is a gene whose knockdown shifts the cell toward a stronger effector state — the target class that checkpoint-blockade therapy drugs to boost immunity. Our direction axis is an 8-hour transcriptional signature, so functional validation (cytokines, proliferation, killing) is the required next step. We score each candidate across seven evidence axes: causal effect, brake direction, donor consistency, viability, druggability, immune genetics, and clinical precedent.

Effect, direction, donor consistency and viability are computed from the genome-scale CD4⁺ leaderboard; druggability, immune-genetics and clinical precedent are curated from Open Targets, ChEMBL and ClinicalTrials.gov. Reproduce with make figure.

The lead · CBLB

A brake with inhibitors in the clinic.

CBLB is an E3 ubiquitin ligase that restrains T-cell activation. Its knockdown lands in the brake quadrant in our screen, and it converges with the strongest external evidence of any candidate — which is why it's our lead.

LEAD TARGET · CBLB
Druggable, clinically: two oral CBL-B inhibitors in trials — NX-1607 (Ph1) and HST-1011 (Ph1/2).
Human genetics: an autoimmune genetic association (loss-of-function direction consistent with a brake) — so inhibiting CBLB may boost anti-tumor immunity.
Causal + control: a top-decile effect (E-distance 6.4) in the positive/brake direction, and an internal positive control for the method.
Honest caveat: donor-split at n=2 (one of two donors drives the effect) — see the donor plot below.
How we found them · the signed causal map

Effect size alone points at the wrong genes.

Rank the 12,449 knockdowns by causal effect and the biggest hits are the cell's own TCR machinery — the wrong direction to drug. Adding a per-cell direction-of-effect axis flips it: those top effects point down (knockdown cripples the cell), and the drug-relevant candidates surface in the sparse positive quadrant. That map is the discovery engine behind the shortlist.

Rigor · reported honestly

We report what two donors can and can't support.

The machinery axis is unanimous — every evaluated TCR gene is donor-consistent. The brake side is where n=2 shows its limits: CD5 and DGKA are donor-consistent; CBLB, SMAD3 and UBASH3A are driven by one donor (as is the high-effect LAT2). As a group, known brakes are not yet significantly enriched in the positive quadrant (one-sided Mann–Whitney vs background over 29 curated brakes; p = 0.70). So the shortlist is a prioritized set of nominations for the full four-donor cohort — not a finished target list, and we say so.

Open source · MIT

Every target traces back to code.

Built with Claude Science: each result is a versioned artifact, and a reviewer agent checks every claim against what ran — it caught a real statistical bug before a figure. One command regenerates the shortlist and all four figures from a clean clone.

# dependency-free core tests git clone github.com/duanchengchen-oss/brakepoint cd brakepoint/pipeline && make smoke # regenerate the target figures make figure # is the brake quadrant enriched? (honest null) python brake_enrichment.py
